Pulls the posts out from between the corners on that end, so the bay is clear across — no girts, splash or steel below the eave there (leave the end checked above and only the gable closes in). The gable truss becomes a structural free-span truss, engineered to carry the end of the roof. Overhead-door jamb posts on that end stay.

Wall sections

Close in just part of a wall, or hang metal from the roof down a set distance over part of a wall — a windbreak over an open bay. On an end, "Gable + level band" closes the gable and carries the metal on down past the truss to a level bottom edge (6" below the truss unless you say otherwise), so it lines up with a band on the side. Sections add to whatever the wall switches above give you, so leave a wall unchecked and add a section to close in only that stretch.

Interior wall

Posts, girts, splash and steel like an end wall, up under the roof, with the metal on the side that faces the open part of the building. The roof carries on over the open bay — a workshop at one end and a covered bay at the other.

Loft

The joists sit on an LVL ledger screwed to the inside face of the wall posts and run straight out onto a 2x12 beam whose plies sandwich 6x6 posts standing on the concrete. Deeper than the longest joist span gets another beam row. The stair is a straight run of 2x12 stringers and treads against the end of the loft, parallel to the joists, landing on the outer edge; the railing leaves a gap for it.

Lean-tos

A lean-to hangs off one wall of the barn. Give it the projection out from the wall, its pitch, and the eave height at the outside — the height where it meets the barn is worked out from those. On a side wall the ledger always lands 2 ft down from the top of the post, so the eave at the outside is worked out for you instead. Leave Length blank to run the whole wall.

Openings

“From ft” is the distance from the left corner as you stand looking at that wall. Leave it blank to let the tool center them. With an interior wall switched on you can put doors and windows in it too — they go in its finished face, measured from the front side.

Post positions

Every post the layout puts on each wall, measured the same way as From ft on the openings, and each lean-to's outer row measured along the barn wall it hangs on. Corner posts, the jamb posts beside an overhead or sliding door, and a lean-to post lined up on a door behind it stay where they are. Type a distance under any other post to slide it along the wall — it will not go within a foot of the next post or into a door — and clear the box to let the tool put it back. The girts, steel, headers and the whole material list follow the moved post.

Posts

The main setting covers the sidewall and endwall posts, the posts each side of an overhead door and the interior wall. The row of posts on the outside of a lean-to is set on its own, so the barn can be glue-lam with plain 6x6s under the lean-tos. Glue-lams are taken off and priced by the lineal foot, the way Timber Building quotes them; a solid post stays by the piece. Glue-lam sizes are the finished Rigidply dimensions — a 3-ply 2x6 is 4-1/16" x 5-1/4", a 4-ply 2x6 is 5-3/8" x 5-1/4", and the 2x8s run 7" through the wall.

Splash plank & girts
Cupolas

Sits on the ridge, evenly spaced down the length. Decorative — it is set on the finished roof with a base flashing, so nothing is cut out of the trusses or the sheeting.

Kickboards — inside

Boards run horizontally on the inside face of the posts, floor up, on every closed-in wall — and on the stall side of the interior wall when that is switched on. Door openings are deducted. A 2x12 covers 11-1/4", so 4 rows come to 3'9".

Truss carrier

The carrier is screwed to the posts with Simpson Strong-Drive SDWS timber screws, .220" x 5" — three into each post through each ply. Where a butt joint in the carrier lands on a post, that post gets three each side of the joint; where the board runs past, three is all it gets on that side.
